India printed the idea on the bond.

The Government of India sold five rupee Premium Prize Bonds over post office counters: red in 1963, blue in 1964. Five rupees fifty came back after five years. Halfway down the promise sat one line.

No other interest will be payable on the Bond but PRIZES, to be determined by lottery.

Britain’s draws came from ERNIE, a machine designed by Tommy Flowers and Harry Fensom of Colossus. It read the noise in neon tubes, made 2,000 numbers an hour, and took three days over its first draw. The Government Actuary’s Department still tests its output every month.

Portaldot already calls a staking deposit a bond. We only had to finish the sentence.

The reverse

The manner of the lottery

1 Prize of 95% of everything the pool earned not issued
1 Fee of 5% to the keeper who runs the draw not issued
Total this round not issued

  1. You bond.

    Your wallet calls nominationPools.join. Your tPOT moves to Pool 1’s own account, and no person has a key to it. MetaMask can sign this, because V3 runs an Ethereum transaction as a native call.

  2. The pool stakes.

    It nominates a validator and earns a staking reward, counted every 6 hours.

  3. The pool pays no interest.

    Its commission is 100%, payable to one Solidity contract of 1,785 bytes, and the pool has given up the role that could change that. Whatever the contract holds is the prize.

  4. A number nobody knows yet.

    The keeper commits a Merkle root of every bond and names the block it read them at. Five blocks later, a block hash that did not exist at commit time picks the ticket.

  5. One bond wins.

    Anyone can submit the proof, from this page if they like. The contract pays 95% to the winner, and every bond stays in the next draw.

There is no vault of deposits. The only contract here has never held one, so the worst a bug can lose is one prize.

    Limits we are not hiding

    • The keeper can post a false snapshot. It cannot hide one, because anyone can rebuild it. The contract does not stop it.
    • The draw uses a block hash. With two validators on this testnet, an author could withhold a block to change it.
    • A pool holds 32 members and the chain allows 16 pools. More members means more pools or a governance change.
    • Unbonding takes 672 eras here. Nobody can shorten that for you, including us.
